# City Manager → Mayor-Council Transition

Source: `academy-material/Mayor's Academy Materials.pdf` — Commissioners' Order ORD-278-24, the City's Transition FAQ, and LINK nky coverage (May 2025).

## The headline fact

**November 2024 ballot initiative: Covington voters approved switching from City Manager government to Mayor-Council government, 61% in favor.** Effective **January 2027**.

- **City Manager form (current, until 2027):** professional city manager runs day-to-day operations; commissioners are collectively the legislative body.
- **Mayor-Council form (starts 2027):** Council = legislative (ordinances, taxes, budget). Mayor = executive (veto power, personnel, daily affairs, tie-breaking vote in council).

## Legally mandated decisions — deadlines

| Decision | Deadline | Governing law |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of council seats** (KY range: 6–12) | **November 5, 2025** | — |
| **Election method**: at-large vs. district/ward vs. hybrid | **November 5, 2025** | KRS 83A.040 (at-large is default absent a district system); KRS 83A.100(1) (wards allowed) |
| Partisan vs. non-partisan elections | Nov 5, 2025 (same window) | KRS 83A.045 — only a handful of KY's 400+ cities go partisan |

## The Committee on Form of Government Transition

Created by **Commissioners' Order No. ORD-278-24**, passed **December 3, 2024** (signed before Mayor Meyer's term ended). Effective Jan 1, 2025 – Dec 31, 2026.

- **No legislative/executive authority** — recommendations only.
- **9 voting members**, including the **Mayor as Chairperson**. Voting members selected by the Mayor, confirmed by the Board of Commissioners.
- City Commissioners sit as **ex officio non-voting** members.
- Ex officio non-voting staff: City Solicitor (legal/drafting), City Manager (structural insight), City Clerk (documentation/logistics/history).
- Mayor also appoints a **Committee Administrator** (coordinates meetings/comms/research).
- Meets **at least monthly**.

**Reporting deadlines the committee owes:**
- Initial Progress Report — **June 30, 2025**
- Monthly progress reports thereafter
- Recommendation on council size + compensation — **October 17, 2025**
- Comprehensive **Final Report — May 1, 2026**

## Committee's first concrete recommendations (presented May 7, 2025, by Director of External Affairs **Sebastian Torres**)

- **6 council seats**
- **Non-partisan elections**
- **Primary elections** only if 13+ candidates run for one seat
- **At-large representation** (not ward-based)
- City-issued laptops/phones for council members
- **Mayor** gets a vehicle/transport stipend + healthcare + retirement plan
- New **council members do NOT get healthcare/retirement** (current commissioners do — a real loss for the new role)
- **Annual council member salary: $17,787.15**
- **Annual mayor salary: $95,335.76** — set as "the highest allowable under KY law for a city of Covington's size" (a formula, not a fixed dollar figure — Commissioner James Toebbe's proposal, so cost-of-living increases don't require a re-vote)
- Council compensation effective 2027; **mayor's new salary effective 2029** (after the current mayoral term ends)
- Average city department-head salary is ~$128,000 — the new mayor salary is set intentionally below that.
- Current commissioner healthcare costs the city ~$74,000/yr total.

**Peer cities used for salary benchmarking** (per Torres — proximity + largest KY mayor-council cities): Independence, Erlanger, Florence, Georgetown, Elizabethtown, Hopkinsville.

## Public input process — a caution worth knowing

Public input was gathered via in-person fora + an online survey (~200 responses). **City Solicitor Frank Schultz** flagged the survey as containing what he called "certainly a misrepresentation" — some responses appeared fraudulent/duplicate. Mayor Washington asked whether that could rise to a felony. Commissioner Tim Acri also raised concerns about survey integrity. Worth knowing if citing this process as clean/definitive — it wasn't without controversy.
