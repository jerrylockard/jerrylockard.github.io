# NKY Housing Blueprint — 4 Deep-Dive Tools & Case Studies

Source: `academy-material/Mayor's Academy Materials.pdf` — the "Northern Kentucky Housing Blueprint" slide deck (HR&A Advisors + NKY Chamber + NKADD), a deep dive on the 4 top-flagged strategies from [[ACADEMY_HOUSING_NKY_OVERVIEW]]'s 50-strategy menu. Each tool below follows the same shape: what it is → impact model → a Kentucky example → national case studies.

## Tool 1: Housing Fund

**What:** a dedicated pool of public/private/philanthropic money for local housing priorities — new homes, down-payment help, repairs, weatherization. Local control = flexible, less red tape than state/federal money.

**Impact model:** a **$10M regional fund over 5 years** (5%/yr growth) could support:
- **+1,000 new income-aligned homes**
- **+500 homes repaired**
- **+275 new homeowners** via down-payment assistance

Rule of thumb: **$1 of a housing fund leverages $4-8** from other public/private partners.

**Kentucky example — Affordable Housing Trust Fund (AHTF):** administered by **Kentucky Housing Corporation (KHC)**, competitive/proposal-based. Homes must serve families earning **<$37K** (preference for **<$19K**).

**Case studies:**
- **The Catalytic Fund** (Northern Kentucky's own — River Cities, CDFI-certified nonprofit, capitalized 2013): deployed **$33.7M**, advanced 90+ projects, created **1,061 new residential units**, delivered **871,000 sq ft** of commercial space, preserved **91** historic buildings. Local example: **Boone Block Lofts** (Covington) — $3.29M, 9 for-sale townhomes, completed 2016.
- **Greenville Housing Fund, SC** (est. 2017): City committed $2.5M/yr, leveraging $33M+ via bonds. 800+ homes (2021), 1,000+ (2022).
- **Charlotte Housing Opportunity Investment Fund, NC** (2019): raised $53M (beat its $50M goal). Impact: **1,927+ homes** created/preserved, **$343M** total development leveraged.
- **Barnes Housing Trust Fund, Nashville/Davidson Co., TN** (2013): $108M+ invested, leveraged **$1B+** (9:1 ratio), **4,700+ homes**.

## Tool 2: Employer-Assisted Housing

**What:** an employer (or consortium) funds loans/grants/subsidies to defray employees' housing costs near the workplace — no public funding or tax increase required.

**Impact model:** one-time **$5M** employer investment → homes for **+125** employee families. **$5M/yr for 5 years** → **+700 new homes**.

**Kentucky example — UK's Employer-Assisted Housing Program (Lexington):** forgivable loans, 5% of purchase price up to **$15,000**, for down payment/closing/renovation. Requires FT staff, assets <$60,000, home in a designated neighborhood, completed homeownership education.

**Case studies:**
- **Audible (Newark, NJ):** $25,000 one-time down-payment grant, FT employees 2+ yrs tenure, salary ≤$95,000. Clawback: 100% if resign <6mo, 50% if <1yr.
- **Atlanta Police Foundation:** $850/month rent voucher for first responders living in-city, up to 12 months, ~$500K program serving 50+ people.
- **UVM Health Network (Burlington, VT):** master-leases 49% of two buildings (10-yr lease). $9M total investment helped finance **180 units + a 75-child childcare center**.

**Policy lever worth knowing:** KY Senate Bill 59 (proposed 2025) would let nonprofit/religious organizations build affordable housing with local approval.

## Tool 3: Missing Middle Housing (incl. ADUs)

**What:** duplexes/triplexes/fourplexes/townhomes/cottage courts — historically zoned out in most neighborhoods.

**Impact data:** 2023 — 148 duplex/triplex/fourplex units permitted in NKY = **32% of all permitted units that year**. Sustained 10%/yr growth over 5 years → ~115 new homes.

**Kentucky program — KHC's Small Multifamily Affordable Loan (SMAL):** for buildings ≤11 units, rents ≤120% AMI capped at 30% of tenant income. Underused: since 2007, funded only 3 developments (~$817K total).

**ADUs specifically:** if just **1% of NKY's ~118,000 owner-occupied single-family homes** built an ADU → **+1,000 new homes region-wide**. Louisville Metro's rules: 1 ADU/lot, max 800 sqft or 30% of primary structure, **no short-term rentals**, owner must live on-site; Louisville's Planning Office provides free pre-approved ADU plans.

**Case studies:**
- **South Bend, IN:** free pre-approved building plans since 2022 ("Sears Catalog" model). Missing-middle share of new construction rose from 3.9% (2010s) to 23% (2020s so far).
- **Durham, NC "Expanding Housing Choices" (2019):** ended single-family-only zoning; 365 permits in 4 years, including 95 ADUs and 215 new small lots (median sale $348K vs. $605K standard single-family).
- **Louisville's proposed Land Development Code amendment:** would allow 6 middle-housing types in residential zones, capped at ≤60% of any block.

## Tool 4: Workforce Development (construction trades)

**What:** classes, apprenticeships, mentor-protégé programs, work experience — funded by a dedicated Workforce Training Fund (e.g., from closing fees or permit fees).

**Context:** since the 2008-09 recession, skilled-trades shortage in residential construction drives up costs and build times. NAHB estimates the shortage cut national single-family production by **~19,000 homes in 2024** alone.

**Regional institution — Enzweiler Building Institute (NKY, est. 1967):** hands-on training in 9 trades, 2,000+ graduates. Recently restored Covington's Colonial Inn with the Covington Academy for Heritage Trades. (See also [[ACADEMY_ECONOMIC_DEVELOPMENT]] for CAHT's 2024 results.)

**Case study — Colorado Workforce Innovation Funding:** 2021 state HB21-1264, **$35M** total. Colorado Springs Home Builders Assoc worked with 46 high schools; 900+ students completed the program, 300 placed in summer jobs.

## Small Developer Support (Top-10 strategy #47 — detailed spotlight)

Traces the housing shortage back to the 2008-09 recession's flight of small developers and trades workers from the industry. Ideas: pre-development capital, technical assistance for small-scale developers, a regional toolkit for identifying small-scale sites, training local banks/planning staff on small-developer challenges, delayed fees for small-scale projects.
