# Federal Job Losses & Covington's Budget

Source: `academy-material/Mayor's Academy Materials.pdf` — WCPO/LINK nky, July 9, 2025.

## The core fact

The **IRS — Covington's second-largest employer** — cut **750 employees** in late June 2025. That directly cost the city **$1.5M in payroll tax revenue**.

This lands on top of money already lost to remote work: **~$4M already lost** from work-from-home policies generally, because **Kentucky payroll tax is tied to where an employee physically works** — a structural exposure, not a one-time event.

IRS workers affected here were housed in the **Gateway Center**.

## What the city is doing

- Mayor Washington personally meeting with employers to encourage return-to-office.
- Economic Development team "putting them on steroids... shaking every tree possible" (Washington's words) for new job creation to offset the loss.
- The city may need to spend down its **remaining American Rescue Plan (ARP)** funds to cover the 2026 budget shortfall — but that funding source **is gone by 2027**, so it's a bridge, not a fix.
- Stated budget priority if cuts become necessary: **"always public safety" first** — Washington.

## Why this matters for civic/budget literacy

This is a concrete, current example of a structural fiscal vulnerability: Covington's tax base is exposed to (a) federal employment decisions it doesn't control, and (b) a state tax-law quirk (work-from-home erodes payroll tax collection regardless of whether the employer is still headquartered locally). Useful context for understanding budget-related questions or platform positions on local fiscal resilience.
